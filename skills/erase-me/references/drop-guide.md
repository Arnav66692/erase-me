# California DROP guide

> Not legal advice. This guide explains a public government tool in plain language. It was checked against official California sources on 2026-10-21. Rules and screens can change, so confirm on privacy.ca.gov before relying on any detail.

## What DROP is

DROP is the Delete Request and Opt-out Platform run by the California Privacy Protection Agency, which now calls itself CalPrivacy. It comes from the Delete Act, Senate Bill 362 (Chapter 709, Statutes of 2023), codified at California Civil Code 1798.99.80 to 1798.99.89. The detailed rules are in California Code of Regulations, title 11, sections 7600 to 7622, in effect since 2026-01-01.

One free request through DROP tells every data broker registered with CalPrivacy to delete the personal information it holds about you. CalPrivacy says this reaches over 600 registered brokers.

- File at https://consumer.drop.privacy.ca.gov/
- Check status at https://consumer.drop.privacy.ca.gov/dropstatus

## Who can use it

Only California residents. CalPrivacy checks residency before you can submit (11 CCR 7620(a)). It does this through the California Identity Gateway, the state's identity service. You do not need a Gateway account. You can type your details directly or sign in with Login.gov if you already have an account. CalPrivacy says the information you enter for this check is not stored afterward and is not shared with DROP.

If the check says "Unable to verify", use the link to request a review of your eligibility. The rules give you 10 calendar days from the classification to ask for a review. You explain why you are a California resident and give an email address or phone number for contact (11 CCR 7622(a)). CalPrivacy may ask for documents in some cases.

## What you need

Name, date of birth and ZIP code are enough to submit. Everything else is optional, but more details give brokers more chances to find a match.

Optional fields
- Other names, including maiden names
- Email addresses
- Phone numbers
- Mobile advertising IDs (MAIDs)
- Connected TV IDs
- Vehicle identification numbers (VINs)

You can add several of each field except date of birth. CalPrivacy does not require the advertising, TV or vehicle IDs.

## Step by step

1. Go to https://consumer.drop.privacy.ca.gov/ and start a request.
2. Verify California residency through the California Identity Gateway.
3. Build your profile with your name, date of birth and ZIP code, then add any older names, emails and phone numbers you want covered.
4. Review the broker list. You can exclude specific brokers if you want to (Civil Code 1798.99.86(a)(3)).
5. Submit. The confirmation page shows an 8-digit DROP ID. Save it somewhere private and do not share it. You need it to check status. If you lose it, the status page has a recovery link that works by email.
6. Come back later to check status. You may add details or change the request, but no sooner than 45 calendar days after you submitted (11 CCR 7620(d)).

In Erase Me, record the DROP ID as evidence on the DROP ledger item and plan a status check. The person signs in and verifies residency themselves. The agent never handles their Login.gov or Identity Gateway credentials.

## What brokers must do, and by when

- From 2026-08-01, every registered broker must access DROP at least once every 45 days (Civil Code 1798.99.86(c)(1)).
- Within 45 days after receiving a request, the broker must process it and delete all personal information related to you (1798.99.86(c)(1)(A)). The rules say this includes inferences and all personal information tied to the matched identifier, not only the fields you typed in (11 CCR 7613(b)).
- If the broker cannot verify the request, for example because your details match several people, it must treat it as an opt-out of selling or sharing your data within the same 45 days (1798.99.86(c)(1)(B), 11 CCR 7613(a)(2)(B)).
- The broker must tell its service providers and contractors to delete too (1798.99.86(c)(1)(C)).
- After deleting, the broker must keep deleting any new personal information about you at least every 45 days, and may not sell or share new personal information about you (1798.99.86(d)).
- If there is no match today, the broker must keep your request and check newly collected records against it before selling or sharing them (11 CCR 7613(c)). You do not need to resubmit.
- Brokers may not contact you to verify a DROP request (11 CCR 7616(c)).
- Brokers report a status for each request at their next access session (11 CCR 7614).

CalPrivacy expects brokers to finish their first download and upload cycle in November 2026. It warns that status updates can take up to 90 days to appear.

## What the statuses mean

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Pending | The broker has not processed the request yet |
| Deleted | The broker matched you and deleted all non-exempt information |
| Opted-out | No exact match, so the broker keeps the data but may not sell or share it |
| Exempted | The broker holds data about you but all of it falls under a legal exemption |
| Record not found | No match. Adding more names, emails or phone numbers may help |

## Filing for a family member

The Delete Act requires DROP to support authorized agents (Civil Code 1798.99.86(b)(8)). CalPrivacy's help pages say you may submit a request for another California resident, for example a parent for a child with permission, or a family member for an elderly relative.

The rules for this are short and strict (11 CCR 7621).
- The person's own California residency must be verified first. You verify the residency of the person the request is for, not your own.
- Before submitting, the person or the agent must enter the agent's full name and email address, and a trade name if the agent is a business, in the person's DROP account.
- An agent may not cancel the request unless the person clearly tells them to.

Erase Me only helps with someone else's request when that person has agreed, and records that consent first. For direct requests to businesses outside DROP, a business may ask the agent for proof of signed permission and may ask the person to confirm directly, unless the agent holds a power of attorney under Probate Code 4121 to 4130 (11 CCR 7063).

## What DROP does not cover

- **People outside California.** Residents of other states and countries cannot use it.
- **Unregistered brokers.** DROP reaches only brokers registered with CalPrivacy. A people-search site that has not registered will not get your request.
- **First-party data.** Information you gave directly to a business, such as an email used to sign up for offers, is not covered (11 CCR 7613(b)(1)(A)). Use a direct CCPA delete request for those businesses.
- **Publicly available information.** CalPrivacy lists publicly available data as outside DROP. The CCPA definition of personal information excludes information lawfully available from government records and some information made public by you or by widely distributed media (Civil Code 1798.140(v)(2)). A listing built from public records may stay up, so also use the site's own opt-out.
- **Exempt data.** Brokers may keep data covered by legal exemptions, for example data regulated by the Fair Credit Reporting Act or the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act (Civil Code 1798.99.80(c), 1798.99.86(c)(2)).
- **Search engines and social media.** DROP does not remove search results or posts. Use Google's and Bing's removal tools and each platform's own reporting.

## Where to complain

If a broker ignores DROP after its deadline, CalPrivacy takes complaints at https://cppa.ca.gov/webapplications/complaint. CalPrivacy enforces the Delete Act but does not act as your lawyer.
